An Assessment of Winter Storm Damage at Telscombe Cliffs Using UAV-SfM Photogrammetry

Keywords: UAV Photogrammetry, Structure-from-Motion (SfM), DEM Differencing, Coastal Geomorphology

Overview: This independent research assessed the impact of winter storm wave energy on short-term cliff erosion along a 154-metre reach of the Brighton to Newhaven Cliffs SSSI on the East Sussex coast. Standard long-term retreat calculations often miss event-driven failures; this project successfully quantified geomorphic changes at specific spatial and temporal scales.

Methodology & Tech Stack:

  • Executed monthly aerial surveys utilizing a DJI S1000 UAV paired with a Nikon D810 camera to generate high-density point clouds and overlapping imagery.
  • Processed datasets through a Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline to produce high-resolution Digital Surface Models (DSMs).
  • Conducted DEM Differencing (Sequential DSM subtraction) to isolate volumetric erosion patterns, applying a strict Limit of Detection (LoD) threshold of ±0.07 m to completely filter out sensor noise.
  • Integrated hydrodynamic data from the Channel Coastal Observatory (Seaford Wave Buoy and Newhaven Tide Gauge) to run correlation analyses between cumulative wave energy and physical cliff retreat.

Key Findings: The data revealed a critical temporal lag in coastal dynamics. While simultaneous monthly wave energy showed almost no mathematical relationship to immediate erosion (R² = 0.0117), antecedent storm energy was strongly correlated with erosion in the following month (R² = 0.6669). This proved that repeated wave energy at the cliff toe progressively undercuts the chalk mass, leading to delayed structural collapse and secondary mass wasting in subsequent weeks.
